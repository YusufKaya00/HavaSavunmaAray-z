## 2025-03-05 - Avoiding Large Object Heap (LOH) Fragmentation in C# Video Processing
**Learning:** In C#, byte arrays larger than 85,000 bytes are allocated on the Large Object Heap (LOH). In this project's WinForms application (`tasarımdeneme3/Form1.cs`), the `ReadSharedMemory` loop was allocating two ~1.2MB byte arrays (`new byte[FrameWidth * FrameHeight * 3]`) and instantiating two `MemoryMappedViewAccessor` objects *per frame* at ~60fps. This causes massive LOH fragmentation and extreme Garbage Collection (GC) pressure, which can severely stutter the application and lead to OutOfMemoryExceptions over time because the LOH is not automatically compacted.
**Action:** When working with high-frequency streams (like uncompressed video frames via `MemoryMappedFiles` or sockets), ALWAYS pre-allocate large byte arrays and object accessors *outside* the polling loop. Reuse the pre-allocated buffers with operations like `accessor.ReadArray(...)` to keep LOH allocations at exactly zero during the critical loop.
